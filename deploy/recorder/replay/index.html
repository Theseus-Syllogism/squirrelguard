Legend
  • Path: where the person most likely was, coloured by time, blue at the start to red at the end.
  • Dashed, faded path: no detection in those frames (missed or hidden); the position is filled in by the motion model.
  • Ground ellipses: the 95 % region for the position at that moment. Bigger where less is known (far away, during gaps).
  • Footprints (green L, blue R): placed from the walking speed and step rate, not seen in the video. Faded = estimated.
  • Orange cone: a guess of the next 2.5 s. It widens quickly because a person can turn at any time.
  • Aerial photo: USGS NAIP (public domain) under the path, lined up by the map calibration; grid squares are 1 m.
  • View cone: the way the head was facing (from nose, eyes and ears; about ±25°), not eye gaze. Bright = central 60°, faint = the 120° a person can see.
  • Stick figure: rough 3D pose from the keypoints, standing on the path; faded while filled in.
  • 3D body: a full body shape (SMPL). On rig replays it is dressed onto the IK rig (same position, bone lengths and joint angles, every calibrated camera); on older samples it was fitted offline by WHAM. Legs that were out of every camera's view are guessed.
  • 3D body joints: the joints of the fitted body, drawn like the stick figure.
  • Camera: where the Wyze camera is and what it sees (frustum). Grid squares are 1 m.
  • Zone: a named ground area (time spent in it is in the readout).
  • Grey line: true path (synthetic data only).
  • Video cut-out: the person as the camera filmed them at that moment, pasted on a flat card standing on the path and turned towards the camera. Exact in Camera view; from the side it is a paper figure.
  • Faded cut-outs: earlier moments (trail) or the whole visit at once (ghosts).
  • Camera projection: the camera's own picture, with the person removed, laid on the ground. Right for flat ground; anything standing up (trees, walls, cars) is smeared away from the camera.
  • Backdrop: a curved screen 30 m from the camera holding what is above the horizon (sky, tree tops, far houses).
  • Splat scene: a 3D scan of the place (Gaussian splatting), lined up with the camera by hand. Turn on Splat + projection to check the alignment.
